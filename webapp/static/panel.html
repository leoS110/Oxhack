How it knows

Weather comes from the : possible futures for every hour and field. Grain moisture follows each future from the latest meter reading, using Henderson's equilibrium moisture, Arrhenius drying rates and Smith, Bailey & Ingram's (1981) rain rule.

An hour's value per hectare is the grain price, minus the drying charge and weight loss at the store (Camgrain's 2026 charges), minus crop lost the longer it stands. Zero when it rains more than  mm in the hour or the grain is over %. The plan picks a field for each combine every hour and is redone every hours, when a new moisture reading arrives.

What it doesn't know yet

  • The drying constants come from dryer tests at 40–60 °C, not standing crop. They need calibrating against meter readings in the field.
  • Dew, sunshine and wind aren't modelled. Oilseed rape constants are borrowed from wheat and barley.
  • The fields on the plan are groups of real fields around Elveden, Suffolk, traced from this imagery: fields,  ha, one crop per group. A combine is sent to a group, not to one field in it, and driving inside a group is free. Driving between groups is the straight line × 1.3 at 12 mph.
